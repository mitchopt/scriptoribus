# Renarratio

**Category:** progymnasmata

**Summary:** Retell a fable or narrative in your own words.

**Description:** The first (fable) and second (narrative) elements respectively of the ancient 'Progymnasmata'; a set of fourteen exercises designed to prepare students of Rhetoric. See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progymnasmata

# Refutatio / Confirmatio

**Category:** progymnasmata

**Summary:** Argue that a story is either credible or implausible

**Description:** The fifth and sixth elements respectively of the ancient 'Progymnasmata'; a set of fourteen exercises designed to prepare students of Rhetoric. See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progymnasmata

# Comparatio

**Category:** progymnasmata

**Summary:** Compare point by point two things or persons.

**Description:** The tenth element of the ancient 'Progymnasmata'; a set of fourteen exercises designed to prepare students of Rhetoric. See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progymnasmata

# Descriptio

**Category:** progymnasmata

**Summary:** Describe an object, image, or person in detail

**Description:** The twelfth element of the ancient 'Progymnasmata'; a set of fourteen exercises designed to prepare students of Rhetoric. See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progymnasmata

# Amplificatio

**Category:** artes poetriae

**Summary:** Expand or 'amplify' a sentence using targeted constructions

**Description:** A staple composition drill in the Middle Ages. See *Poetria Nova*, Geoffrey of Vinsauf.

# Abbreviatio

**Category:** artes poetriae

**Summary:** Compress an elaborate passage into a simpler one.

**Description:** The paired opposite of *amplificatio*. A staple composition drill in the Middle Ages. See *Poetria Nova*, Geoffrey of Vinsauf.

# Dictamen

**Category:** ars dictaminis

**Summary:** Compose a letter in conformity to a template

**Description:** An example template: i. greeting, ii. praise of the recipient, iii. narration of facts, iv. make a petition, v. conclusion/farewell.

# Quaestio Disputata

**Category:** scholasticus

**Summary:** Argue for or against a statement.

**Description:** Classical university disputation. Propose a yes/no question, enumerate some objections, then reply point by point. See, for example, *Summa Theologiae*, St. Thomas Aquinas. Note: for the sake of this exercise, the proposals do not need to be sophisticated!

# Double Translation

**Category:** translatio

**Summary:** Translate a passage of Latin into English (or, if you prefer to avoid translation, start with an existing translation). Wait some amount of time, then translate the English back into Latin. Compare with the original.

**Description:** This exercise appears in some Humanist educational treatises. See for example, *The Scholemaster*, Roger Ascham. See also the Jesuit *Ratio Studiorum*.

# Copia

**Category:** variatio

**Summary:** Rewrite a single sentence in as many different ways as possible.

**Description:** See *De Copia*, Erasmus. He famously gives 195 unique variations on the sentence "Your letter delighted me greatly" (tuae litterae me magnopere delectarunt).

# Dictogloss

**Category:** variatio

**Summary:** Listen to a short Latin passage 1–3 times, then reconstruct from memory.

**Description:** A typical exercise in modern language classrooms. The text should be encountered at a normal speed. Often students take simple notes as they listen. Then the student prepares a summary of the passage. See https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictogloss

# Lipogram

**Category:** scriptio constricta

**Summary:** Write something while avoiding a certain letter

**Description:** Composition avoiding certain letters is an ancient practice. See for example Lasus of Hermione's *Hymn to Demeter*, which entirely avoids the letter 'Sigma'.
